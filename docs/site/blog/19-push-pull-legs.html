Push Pull Legs: The PPL Split Explained (With a Simple Workout Plan)

Push pull legs is one of the most popular ways to organize your training, and for good reason: it's simple, it works for beginners and for experienced lifters, and you can run it 3 or 6 days a week. Here's what a PPL split is, a straightforward workout for each day, and how to schedule it.

What is push pull legs?

Push pull legs (PPL) is a workout split that groups exercises by movement:

DayMusclesMovement
PushChest, shoulders, tricepsPushing weight away from you
PullBack, rear shoulders, bicepsPulling weight toward you
LegsQuads, hamstrings, glutes, calvesSquatting, hinging, lunging

Because muscles that work together are trained on the same day, each group gets a full session and then a few days to recover before you hit it again.

Start with a weight you can lift with good form for the full range of reps. When you hit the top of the range on every set, add a little weight next time.

Beginners: start with 3 days. More experienced: 6 days hits every muscle twice a week, but only if you sleep and recover well. A 4- or 5-day rotation (push, pull, legs, rest, repeat) sits in between.

Is push pull legs good?

  • Pros: simple to follow, flexible (3 to 6 days), every muscle group gets a focused session, easy to track progress.
  • Cons: on 3 days a week each muscle is trained only once a week; on 6 days it takes a lot of time and recovery.

Push pull legs FAQ

Is push pull legs good for beginners?

Yes. Run it 3 days a week (push, pull, legs with a rest day in between) and focus on learning the exercises with a light weight.

How many days a week should I do push pull legs?

3 days for beginners, 4 to 5 for most people, and 6 if you recover well and have the time.

What is the 3-3-3 rule in the gym?

There's no single official version, but it usually means a simple routine of 3 exercises, 3 sets each, 3 times a week. It's a beginner-friendly way to start, and it fits nicely into a 3-day push pull legs week.

Can I do cardio on a push pull legs split?

Yes. Easy cardio fits on rest days or after push and pull days. Keep hard cardio away from legs day if you want to squat well.
